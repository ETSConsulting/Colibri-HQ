About

The voyage, the city, the hidden language

Colibrí began on the water. Years spent as a gondolier taught a first, wordless lesson: that a song can carry a stranger across a threshold. The slow pull of the oar, the echo beneath a bridge, the hush before light breaks on the far side — these were the first grammar of a language older than any city.

Because the music was never only Venetian. Beneath the Italian melodies run Irish laments and Brazilian rhythms, Romanian myths and songs that borders were never able to hold.

Between Two Worlds

Every passage through those ancient archways was a crossing from one world into another — shadow into gold, stone into sky. The baroque facades, the fracture of light through old glass, the echo of footsteps across marble: these were never just scenery. They became instruments in their own right.

Drawing from the intertwined traditions of Celtic manuscript illumination and Venetian architecture, the work lives in the spaces where ornament becomes structure and beauty becomes meaning — the same devotion that once drove a scribe to spend a year on a single painted letter.

The Hidden Language

Underneath the Italian, Irish, Brazilian, and Romanian songs runs the same current: a lingua arcana, a hidden language that lives in every heart long before it learns a single word. The same archetypes — the traveler, the lover, the mourner, the fool — singing in four tongues across the same deep water.

The Name

Colibrí — the hummingbird — is a creature that moves between worlds, hovering in the space between stillness and flight. Like the music, it never quite lands. It just keeps moving, iridescent, toward the light.
